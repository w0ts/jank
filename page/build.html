Build a jank mini 3

The jank mini 3 is a Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-2 (the ESP32-S3 with its 2 inch colour screen) on our board, in a printed box. Our board brings the MIDI outs (TRS + DIN each), the clock and reset outs, the line in, the USB port for gear and the D-pad. The LCD-2 sits in our board's notch; two small bridge strips join its pins to ours from below. Nothing is soldered between them: the LCD-2 stays removable. A 2000 mAh LiPo cell lies on the case floor.

The steps: buy the LCD-2 and the other parts, order our board and the strips from JLCPCB, print the case, put it together, install the firmware. The design files: hardware/jank-mini-3/ (its README, the schematic, the case's BOX.md).

Order the board from JLCPCB

Two ways to have it made. The board is the same; the files differ in what JLCPCB solders.

JLCPCB solders everything: the small parts on top, the two USB-C sockets J1 / J4, and under the board the DIN sockets, the jacks and the header rows J15 / J16 (Standard assembly, both sides). No soldering on the board for you.

The files (folder fab/full/)

  • gerbers.zip: the board (upload it as it is).
  • bom.csv: the parts list (BOM).
  • cpl.csv: where each part goes (pick and place, CPL).

Step by step

  1. Open the quote page. On cart.jlcpcb.com/quote, press Add gerber file and pick this way's gerbers.zip (as it is, do not unzip it).
  2. Check what JLCPCB found. Both sides of the board appear, and the line under them reads Detected 2 layer board of 82x95.5mm.
  3. Layers 2, quantity 5. JLCPCB fills in the layers and the size from the files. Leave Base Material at FR-4 and PCB Qty at 5.
  4. Thickness 1.6 mm, Surface Finish LeadFree HASL. JLCPCB picks OSP by default: change it. Leave the colour and the rest at the defaults.
  5. Outer Copper Weight: 1 oz (the default; the board's tracks go down to 0.10 mm).
  6. Confirm Production file: Yes. JLCPCB then shows you what its CAM did to J1 / J4's rings and where its tooling holes go, before it makes the boards. It asks you to confirm within 48 hours: press Confirm, and leave Do not confirm automatically unticked.
  7. PCB Remark. Open Advanced Options, press the pencil next to PCB Remark and paste: 
    J1 / J4 GCT USB4085: 0.40 mm holes in 0.65 mm pads and 0.44-0.45 mm hole spacing as in the maker's recommended layout; please do not enlarge the pads or reduce the holes; breakout acceptable
  8. Switch on PCB Assembly. Then:
    • tick Assemble bottom side too (the DINs, the jacks and J15 / J16 are underneath),
    • PCBA Type: Standard (Economic cannot assemble both sides),
    • Assembly Side: Both Sides,
    • PCBA Qty 5, Edge Rails at Added by JLCPCB.
    JLCPCB then says the board grows to 95.5 x 92 mm: it adds two 5 mm edge rails, which come off the boards after (the form offers JLCPCB's depaneling service for that).
  9. Press NEXT and sign in. The steps after this one need a JLCPCB account. (On this panel the assembly lines stay empty: the parts are priced once the BOM is in.)
  10. Upload the BOM and the CPL. bom.csv as the BOM, cpl.csv as the pick and place file, then Process BOM & CPL.
    Photo to come: JLCPCB's BOM and CPL upload (fully assembled)
  11. Check the parts. Every line of the BOM should find its part.
    Photo to come: JLCPCB's parts list, every part found (fully assembled)
  12. Check the placement of every part. J1 / J4's position and rotation, and the rotation of every part under the board: those come from KiCad as they are.
    Photo to come: JLCPCB's placement preview, the bottom side with the DINs, jacks and J15 / J16
  13. Save to cart and order. Pay, and JLCPCB makes, assembles and ships the boards.

When the boards arrive: solder

  1. J1 / J4 first, from below. Their signal tails end flush with the board's bottom; the front shell legs stop 0.8 mm short of it: fill those slots with solder. A fine tip and flux.
    Photo to come: J1 and J4 soldered, seen from below
  2. Meter them before going on: J4's VUSB against GND and against CC1O, J1's VBUS pairs against GND, neighbouring pins against each other (no shorts), and D+ / D- continuity from J1 through U5 to J4.
  3. The DINs and the jacks (J2, J3, J5, J6, J7, J13, J14), under the board.
    Photo to come: the board from below with the DINs and the jacks soldered
  4. J15 / J16 with the LCD-2 as a jig. Put the headers into our board from below, plug a strip and the LCD-2 on, and solder them like that: the 1.2 mm holes would let a loose header sit ~0.3 mm off its row. No cell and no USB on the LCD-2 while you do this. Trim the tails under the board after.
    Photo to come: J15 / J16 being soldered with a strip and the LCD-2 plugged on as the jig

Order the bridge strips

Each device takes two strips, the same strip twice (turned 180 degrees it serves the other row). A strip is a 37.06 x 10 mm board with two 1 x 14 sockets: one takes the LCD-2's pins, the other our J15 / J16. A separate JLCPCB order, 10 strips for 5 devices, 1.6 mm thick. Two ways again:

  • Bare, the PCB only: strip/fab/bare/gerbers.zip. You solder 2 sockets C2905420 into each strip (they are in lcsc-order.txt; with the fully assembled board, buy 2 per strip from LCSC).
  • Assembled, both sockets fitted by JLCPCB (Economic): gerbers.zip, bom.csv, cpl.csv (folder strip/fab/assembled/).
  1. Upload the strip's gerbers.zip on the quote page. JLCPCB reads Detected 2 layer board of 10x37.06mm.
  2. PCB Qty 10, thickness 1.6 mm. (The files name no surface finish for the strip; these screenshots use LeadFree HASL, like the board.)
  3. Assembled strips only: switch on PCB Assembly, Economic, Top Side, PCBA Qty 10; then NEXT, sign in, upload the strip's bom.csv and cpl.csv. Bare strips: no assembly, press SAVE TO CART.

Print the case

Three parts: the shell (the face and the four walls), the flat lid underneath, and the D-pad cap. The face's labels and the cap's symbols are inlaid in a second colour (a multi-material printer).

Bambu Studio projects (ready to slice)

  • box-shell-0.2.3mf: the shell, black, face down, the labels pink. 0.2 mm nozzle, 0.10 mm Standard (the labels' strokes are about 0.37 mm, under a 0.4 nozzle's line).
  • box-lid-cap-0.4.3mf: plate 1 the lid (black, outside down), plate 2 the D-pad cap (grey, top down, the symbols pink). 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm Standard.

Both: Bambu PLA Basic (filament 1 black, 2 pink, 3 grey), Textured PEI plate. PLA softens from about 55 °C: keep the device out of a hot car and out of the sun.

Or the STLs, for any slicer

  • box-shell.stl + box-shell-labels.stl (the labels, second colour)
  • box-lid.stl
  • box-cap.stl + box-cap-symbols.stl (the symbols, second colour)

They are exported the way the parts sit in the device: turn them in the slicer (the shell face down, the lid outside down, the cap top down), and load each part with its inlays as one multi-part object so they stay aligned. The source: box.scad (OpenSCAD).

Put it together

  1. Before the first power-up: check U19 (CJ431) against LCSC's picture of its pin order (1 REF, 2 K, 3 A), and power the board the first time from a current-limited supply.
  2. The strips (bare ones): solder the two sockets into each strip.
  3. The cell's lead. Solder the PH2.0 pigtail (for the cell's plug) to the MX1.25 2-pin pigtail, red to red, and heat-shrink the joints; the power switch goes in the red wire: cell, switch, MX1.25. MX1.25 pigtail colours vary: meter which pin is + and check it lands on the LCD-2's pin 1 (VBAT) before the first plug-in. Inside the case the red wire runs about 182 mm (cell to switch to the LCD-2), the black about 87 mm: make them long enough, with some slack.
    Photo to come: the adapter lead with the power switch in its red wire
  4. The inserts. 4 x M2 into the bosses under the shell's face, 2 x M3 into the lid's two screw tabs, from the wall side.
    Photo to come: the inserts in the shell's bosses and in the lid's tabs
  5. The LCD-2 on the board. Plug the strips onto the LCD-2's pins and onto J15 / J16 (J15 takes the LCD-2's row P1, J16 the row P2, pin 14 at the USB end). The LCD-2 sits in the notch, its board level with ours.
    Photo to come: the LCD-2 on our board with the two strips
  6. The cap, then the board, into the shell. The D-pad cap goes into its cross-shaped opening from inside (its flange stays under the face). Then the board, with the LCD-2 and the strips on it, drops into the shell from below: 4 screws M2 x 5 through its holes into the bosses. Do this before the power switch is in: with the switch fitted, a screwdriver cannot reach the hole next to it.
    Photo to come: the board screwed into the shell, seen from below
  7. The power switch snaps into the round hole in the left wall.
  8. The cell lies on the lid in the corner under the right part, pushed against the L-shaped wall, with ~2 mm of rubber or foam on its four held sides. The lead: both wires leave the cell's top end, run left in the channel between the DIN sockets and the L's top arm, and down the gap between the strips and the L's left arm; the black goes on under the LCD-2 to its MX1.25, the red to the switch and up along the left wall to the MX1.25. Plug it into the LCD-2 (polarity checked).
    Photo to come: the cell in its corner and the lead routed to the switch and the LCD-2
  9. Close it. Hook the lid's right side in first (tilted), swing the left side up, then the two M3 x 6 countersunk screws sideways through the walls (one in the bottom wall, one in the left wall).
    Photo to come: the finished jank mini 3

The firmware

On the Install page, choose jank mini 3 and flash from the browser. Then run and watch it on the Device page.

USB: the LCD-2's own USB-C (left wall, labelled ESP) is for a computer or a charger. For gear with a USB port (host mode), a short USB-C cable outside the box joins the LCD-2's USB-C to J1 (top wall, ESP), and the gear goes into J4 (DEVICE). J4 gives that gear no power: bus-powered gear needs its own power or a powered hub.
